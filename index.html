WHO GETS TO PLAY?

Musical participation, public music education and examination activity across Australia

Wan Adlan Hakim bin Wan Zalizan · 35572140 · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · Monash University Malaysia

Music-making is shaped by who participates, what people learn, and whether services are available where they live. This story connects national participation patterns with school-age arts exposure, NSW’s publicly funded regional conservatorium network and formal music examination activity.

METHODOLOGY & DEFINITIONS

How to read the evidence

The visualisation combines several official and research datasets. Measures are interpreted within their original survey or administrative context rather than treated as directly equivalent.

MEASURES

Instrument playing ≠ creating music

ABS “singing or playing a musical instrument” and Creative Australia “create music” use different survey questions and populations. They are therefore not treated as the same measure or as a time series.

INTERPRETATION

Association is not causation

The relationship between school-age arts education and later creative participation is an association. The data do not establish that arts education caused the later participation difference.

GEOGRAPHY

Locations are approximate

Regional conservatorium coordinates represent approximate locality or postcode positions and should not be interpreted as exact institution entrances.

PUBLIC FUNDING

Grant values are approvals

Regional Conservatorium figures represent approved grant amounts for the program period, not actual expenditure or operating costs.

PARTICIPATION

Instrument playing is uncommon — and participation differs across groups

ABS data place instrument playing within a wider creative landscape. Participation is highest among younger adults and also differs by gender, reinforcing that “who plays” is not evenly distributed.

LEARNING & ENGAGEMENT

Earlier arts learning is linked with later participation — while age reshapes listening and creation

Creative Australia reports a strong association between school-age arts education and later creative participation. Age also changes how Australians create and consume music.

People taught an artform at school age report more creative participation later

Creative Australia, 2025 · Association does not imply causation

Association, not causation: 20.8% of people taught an artform reported creating music, compared with 7.6% of those not taught an artform.

Source: Creative Australia, National Arts Participation Survey 2025.

04

PUBLIC ACCESS

Regional music education is supported through a distributed public-service network

NSW’s Regional Conservatorium Grants Program supports 17 recipients and is designed to expand music education for schools, students, teachers and communities in regional, rural and remote areas. In 2025–26, the listed approvals totalled $8.64 million.

Public-service context: Program objectives include increasing public-school participation, engaging equity target groups and extending music education to regional, rural and remote locations.

05

FORMAL MUSIC LEARNING

Formal music examination activity grew, with practical assessments dominating

AMEB NSW administered 32,013 examinations in 2024–25, 3% more than the previous reporting period. Practical music assessments accounted for 25,403 examinations across repertoire, comprehensive and leisure formats.

AMEB NSW examination activity rose 3%

Total examinations, 2023–24 to 2024–25

Trend: Total examinations increased from 30,980 to 32,013.

Source: NESA Annual Report 2024–25, AMEB NSW.

Two-thirds of practical assessments were face-to-face

AMEB NSW practical music assessments, 2024–25

Delivery: 16,806 practical assessments were face-to-face and 8,597 were submitted by video.

Source: NESA Annual Report 2024–25, AMEB NSW.

Repertoire was the largest practical music examination format

AMEB NSW practical music examinations, 2024–25

Hierarchy: Repertoire accounted for 11,016 practical music examinations, followed by comprehensive (8,860) and leisure (5,527).

Source: NESA Annual Report 2024–25, AMEB NSW.
